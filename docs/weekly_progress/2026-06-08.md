## Slide 1 — HY-World 2.0 overview

Inputs include text, a single image, multiview images and video.

- Generation output: a navigable 3D world represented as 3DGS plus mesh.
- Reconstruction output: 3DGS or point cloud in one WorldMirror 2.0 feed-forward pass.

## Slide 2 — Four-stage generation pipeline

1. **HY-Pano 2.0:** prompt → seamless 360° panorama.
2. **WorldNav:** collision-free camera-path planning.
3. **WorldStereo 2.0:** camera-controlled keyframe generation.
4. **WorldMirror 2.0 + 3DGS:** fuse the keyframes into a 3DGS scene.

## Slide 3 — Single-image generation

- Inputs/outputs shown: one image, panorama and 3DGS.
- Reported runtime: about one hour locally without limits, or about 20 minutes online with a 20-generation-per-day limit.

## Slide 4 — Feed-forward reconstruction

- WorldMirror 2.0: approximately 1.2B parameters, one pass, under one minute.
- VGGT-Ω: approximately 1B parameters, one pass, under one minute.

## Slide 5 — PanoWorld-LRM

Text, a 2D floor plan and a style are converted to panoramas, poses and room IDs.

## Slide 6 — Simulation

The deck contrasts mesh-based simulators with Habitat-GS:

- mesh rasterization has limited visual realism and weaker transfer to messy real scenes;
- Habitat-GS offers view-dependent 3DGS rendering, drivable Gaussian avatars and Habitat ecosystem compatibility;
- InteriorGS is cited as a pool of 1,000 scenes.

## Slide 7 — Navigation models

- Kachaka mobile robot and its Python/ROS 2 control layer.
- Camera, trajectory and command topics were ready.
- NoMaD was selected as the first navigation model to test.
